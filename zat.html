The whole film turns on one rule: the woman in the red blouse is never a ghost on screen.

1. She looks entirely human in every single shot. Solid, warm, weighted, lit exactly like the villagers around her. No pallor, no translucency, no glow, no damage, nothing floating. In shot 12 her shoulder is physically touching the man beside her and he is fine. The horror is that she is ordinary — the audience should spend half the film unsure whether anything is wrong at all. Everything that is wrong is contextual, never anatomical: an empty ground at 2am (1), wet grave soil on a dry chair (22), and only at the very end her feet (50).

2. The old audience are silhouettes, not corpses. In 33 and 57 nobody is injured, decayed or distorted. Plain clothes of an earlier generation, sitting perfectly still, not clapping, back-lit with faces unresolved. If a face comes out sharp and monstrous, throw the image away — the fear is the stillness and the clothes.

3. Two leading ladies, deliberately different. Ma Thida (13–20, 38) and Ma Nwe (43–54) wear the same class of costume in different colours and must read as two people. Ma Nwe is barefoot in every cemetery shot — that is the tell that she walked out there.

4. Get the hsaing waing right. The stage plate names it: the pat waing is twenty-one tuned drums hung inside a tall circular gilded frame with mirror-glass inlay — not a drum kit, not a single barrel drum. Beside it the kyi waing gong circle, a rack of maung gongs, the hne oboe, si and wa. It sits at the side of the stage and should be visible in most stage-wide frames.

5. Period discipline. 1990s upper Myanmar, Sagaing dry zone. Incandescent bulbs on twisted wire, a valve amplifier and horn speakers, hand-painted canvas backdrops. No phones, no LED, no printed banners, no modern shoes or fabric prints. Dry-zone country — tamarind, toddy palms, cracked pale earth — never delta green.

The front row must match. Shots 5, 8, 11, 33 and 57 are the same framing from the stage. Reuse the ပွဲခင်း plate on all of them so the audience recognises the seats.
