This is a medical thriller, not a ghost story. It should look like a hospital drama for its first eighty shots.

1. Dr Than Htun must look trustworthy. He is the villain, and the whole film depends on the audience liking him first. Calm, warm, immaculate, the senior doctor every family wants. No menace anywhere in his face until shot 61, where the smile finally goes. He is never lit from below, never in shadow, never sneering. He argues his case sincerely in the basement — shot 75 is genuine grief for his own daughter.

2. The dead are ordinary patients, not monsters. They appear only in shots 94, 96 and 97. Solid, warm-skinned, uninjured, no blood, no pallor, no translucency, feet flat on the floor, standing still in hospital gowns. If a face comes out ghoulish, throw the image away — the whole point is that they look like people who should still be alive.

3. Two visual worlds. Shots 1–70 are cold, clean and modern: fluorescent panels, pale vinyl, stainless steel, monitor glow. Shots 71–99 and 103–104 are the room below — dull red light on bare concrete. Nothing gothic anywhere: no cobwebs, no decay, no candles. The only occult objects in the entire film are a brass bowl, two paper slips and a stick of incense, all in 88.

4. Nothing readable, ever — this one is strict. The story runs on records, so screens and paper are everywhere: charts (25, 30, 46), the CCTV footage (40–42), the phone message (68), the file (83), the donor slips (72, 88) and the final record (109, 110). Every one must be angled away, blurred, glared out or shot from behind. Shot 42 especially: the mark on the door must read as an abstract red shape, NOT as legible characters — the number belongs in the narration, not on screen.

5. Ko Kyaw Zin keeps his dressing. The small gauze square on his forehead in 26 is still there in 96. It is how the audience recognises him among twenty gowns.

6. No gore. The resuscitation in 18 has no blood. The body in 103 is covered, and only a hand shows — a hand far older than sixty-two, undamaged.

The mother is the spine of the film. Shots 9, 11, 52, 53, 59, 63, 65, 100 and 108. Keep her warm and specific; the ending only lands if the audience would miss her.

Shots 40, 41 and 42 carry no reference on purpose. They are security-camera frames — grainy black-and-white, overhead, interference banding — and should look nothing like the rest of the film. Generate them free-standing and resist making them pretty.
