One mother. One reference. This is the whole film.

The two mothers must be indistinguishable, so there is a single mother plate and every shot of either version attaches it. Giving them separate references would be the fastest way to break the story — two references drift, and the moment the audience can tell them apart the ending stops landing.

There are exactly two differences, and they are carried by the shot prompts, never by the reference:
  • Dry or soaked. Shots 10–16 are the impossible one — walking through a downpour with dry hair, dry cloth, clean feet. Shots 33, 34, 41 and 43 are the real one, drenched.
  • Scar or no scar. A thin pale burn on the RIGHT wrist. Present in shot 28, absent in shot 45, and back in shot 49 twelve years later in exactly the same place. Those three macros have to match each other precisely — they are the reveal.

Neither version is ever monstrous. No pallor, no strange eyes, no cold grade, nothing uncanny. She looks exactly like their mother because she is their mother. The dread is entirely in small wrong details: the dry clothes, the dog hiding, the left hand, asking her own daughter's age.

Shot 39 is the emotional turn — the first time she looks openly sorrowful. Grieving, not threatening. If it reads as menace the ending is lost.

Shot 55 is the answer. Same blouse, same htamein, dry, in the rain — and walking toward the house, not away. That direction is the story.
